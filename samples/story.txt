My boss offered me a promotion if I helped him fire my best friend.

He called me into his office Friday afternoon. On his desk was the senior position I’d spent two years working toward. Beside it was a written warning with Sarah’s name on it.

“The client’s threatening to leave,” he said. “Someone has to take responsibility.”

Sarah had spent three nights finishing that proposal. I knew because I’d stayed late helping her. She’d submitted it on time.

Our boss had forgotten to forward it.

“I just need you to confirm she delivered it late.”

I stared at him.

“That isn’t what happened.”

He closed the promotion folder.

“Then perhaps you’re not ready for more responsibility.”

That evening, Sarah texted me.

“He’s asked me to meet with HR Monday. Am I getting fired?”

I typed three different replies before putting my phone down.

Monday morning, he brought me into the meeting too.

Sarah sat beside an unopened notebook. She always took notes. That morning, she couldn’t hold her pen still.

Our boss explained that her missed deadline had damaged the client relationship. Then he turned to me.

“You were working with her. Tell them.”

I opened my laptop. Sarah looked down.

“She submitted it Thursday at eight forty-three,” I said.

I shared the email. Underneath was his reply.

“Excellent work. I’ll forward this tonight.”

HR asked him why that hadn’t been included in his report. For once, he had nothing prepared.

The warning was withdrawn. After an investigation, he was dismissed.

Two weeks later, HR reopened applications for his position. Sarah walked over to my desk.

“I’m applying,” she said.

I smiled.

“Good. I’d like a boss I can trust.”
